  1. Article  ·  Corporate Governance  ·  Artificial Intelligence

    Algorithmic Boardrooms and the Business Judgment Rule: Reimagining Director Fiduciary Duties in the Era of Autonomous Corporate Systems

    Dr. Arundhati Sen & Vikramaditya Nair

    Examines whether the classical business judgment rule can meaningfully insulate directors who rely upon black-box computational analytics for capital allocation and strategic M&A valuation, proposing an augmented standard of 'algorithmic procedural due diligence'. The article surveys SEBI's LODR mandates, MCA's enhanced reporting obligations, and comparative governance codes in the UK and Delaware to construct a framework that preserves accountability while accommodating machine agency in boardroom decision-making.

    Vol. 1, Issue 1 (2026)  ·  pp. 1–38 DOI: 10.1000/jcfl.2026.01.01

  2. Article  ·  Insolvency & Restructuring  ·  International Law

    Harmonizing Cross-Border Insolvency: Institutional Readiness and Creditor Priority Under the Proposed UNCITRAL Adaptation

    Prof. Raghavendra Joshi

    Critically assesses the draft framework adapting the UNCITRAL Model Law on Cross-Border Insolvency for Indian jurisprudence, examining COMI determination principles, the public policy carve-out, and creditor hierarchy before the NCLT. The paper draws on recent SAT and High Court precedents to argue for a modified recognition doctrine that preserves domestic creditor protections while enabling efficient multinational restructuring.

    Vol. 1, Issue 1 (2026)  ·  pp. 39–72 DOI: 10.1000/jcfl.2026.01.02
    Read →

  3. Essay  ·  Securities Regulation  ·  FinTech

    Fractional Capital and Tokenized Securities: Regulatory Perimeter of SEBI and the Digital Asset Conundrum

    Ananya Deshmukh

    Interrogates SEBI's CIS regulations, SM-REIT amendments, and custody mandates as applied to fractional tokenization models for real estate and listed securities. Argues for an activity-based regulatory framework that distinguishes between ownership tokens, profit-sharing tokens, and utility tokens, and proposes a sandbox regime for SEBI-supervised digital asset pilots.

    Vol. 1, Issue 1 (2026)  ·  pp. 73–98 DOI: 10.1000/jcfl.2026.01.03
    Read →

  4. Article  ·  Competition & Markets  ·  Digital Regulation

    From Ex-Post Penalties to Ex-Ante Prescriptions: Evaluating the Draft Digital Competition Bill and Systemically Significant Digital Enterprises

    Siddharth Chawla

    Evaluates proposed ex-ante behavioral obligations for Systemically Significant Digital Enterprises under India's Draft Digital Competition Bill, with comparative reference to the EU Digital Markets Act and UK Digital Markets, Competition, and Consumers Act. Examines the designation criteria, self-preferencing prohibitions, and interoperability mandates, arguing that the threshold-based approach underestimates ecosystem lock-in effects in multi-sided platform markets.

    Vol. 1, Issue 1 (2026)  ·  pp. 99–134 DOI: 10.1000/jcfl.2026.01.04
    Read →

  5. Note  ·  ESG & Sustainability  ·  Capital Markets

    Greenwashing in the Capital Markets: BRSR Core Verification, ESG Rating Provider Accountability, and the Limits of Voluntary Corporate Disclosures

    Meera Krishnan

    Analyses SEBI's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR) Core framework and the registration regime for ESG Rating Providers introduced through the Master Circular, examining the legal remedies available against greenwashing under SEBI fraud and misrepresentation provisions and the Companies Act, 2013.

    Vol. 1, Issue 1 (2026)  ·  pp. 135–158 DOI: 10.1000/jcfl.2026.01.05

  6. Case Comment  ·  Mergers & Acquisitions  ·  Securities Regulation

    Creeping Control, Interlocking Shareholdings, and the Fiduciary Duties of Target Boards Under the SEBI Takeover Code

    Devendra Singhania

    Examines recent contested corporate control battles before the Securities Appellate Tribunal and the Supreme Court of India, critically analysing the neutrality rule applicable to target boards under Regulation 26 of the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011. Critiques the proportionality analysis applied in exemption orders and proposes a calibrated fiduciary framework.

    Vol. 1, Issue 1 (2026)  ·  pp. 159–182 DOI: 10.1000/jcfl.2026.01.06
